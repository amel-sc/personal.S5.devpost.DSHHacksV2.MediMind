Academic stress

Definition
A physical and psychological reaction to a workload perceived as excessive or to academic demands (exams, assignments, deadlines).

Common signs and symptoms
•⁠  ⁠Muscle tension, headaches, digestive issues
•⁠  ⁠Irritability, nervousness, difficulty relaxing
•⁠  ⁠Feeling overwhelmed or short on time
•⁠  ⁠Difficulty focusing on one task at a time
•⁠  ⁠Fatigue despite adequate sleep

Frequent causes
•⁠  ⁠Heavy or poorly distributed workload
•⁠  ⁠Lack of work method or organization
•⁠  ⁠High expectations (personal, family, institutional)
•⁠  ⁠Exams or approaching deadlines
•⁠  ⁠Lack of sleep or recovery time

Suggested solutions

Organization
•⁠  ⁠Plan revisions in advance with a realistic, visual calendar
•⁠  ⁠Break large assignments into small steps with sub-goals
•⁠  ⁠Use a time-management method (e.g., Pomodoro: 25 min of work, 5 min break)
•⁠  ⁠Prioritize tasks by urgency and importance (Eisenhower matrix)
•⁠  ⁠Prepare materials and workspace in advance to avoid wasted time

Cognitive and emotional
•⁠  ⁠Put the stakes of an exam into perspective relative to the whole academic journey
•⁠  ⁠Replace thoughts like "I'll never manage this" with more realistic statements
•⁠  ⁠Practice deep breathing or heart-rate coherence exercises before an assessment
•⁠  ⁠Visualize a positive outcome for an exam or presentation
•⁠  ⁠Accept that a moderate level of stress is normal and can be motivating

Lifestyle
•⁠  ⁠Alternate intense work periods with real breaks (walking, stretching, music)
•⁠  ⁠Maintain regular physical activity, even briefly
•⁠  ⁠Ensure adequate sleep during exam periods
•⁠  ⁠Limit caffeine and stimulants during periods of heavy workload
•⁠  ⁠Allow yourself moments of rest without guilt

Social
•⁠  ⁠Study in groups to share the load and motivate each other
•⁠  ⁠Talk about your workload with a teacher, tutor, or academic advisor
•⁠  ⁠Ask for help at the first signs of difficulty rather than at the last minute
