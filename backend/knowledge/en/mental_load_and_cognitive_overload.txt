Mental load and cognitive overload

Definition
An accumulation of tasks, responsibilities, and thoughts to manage simultaneously, leading to significant mental fatigue.

Common signs and symptoms
•⁠  ⁠A feeling of "never stopping thinking" about things to do
•⁠  ⁠Frequent forgetfulness despite seemingly good organization
•⁠  ⁠Mental fatigue by the end of the day without significant physical effort
•⁠  ⁠Difficulty relaxing even during free time

Frequent causes
•⁠  ⁠Multiplication of responsibilities (studies, work, personal life)
•⁠  ⁠Lack of external organizational tools (keeping everything "in your head")
•⁠  ⁠Difficulty delegating or prioritizing

Suggested solutions

Externalization
•⁠  ⁠Use written or digital tools to centralize all tasks (a single list, a calendar)
•⁠  ⁠Write down a thought or task immediately rather than keeping it in memory
•⁠  ⁠Do a weekly check-in to reorganize priorities

Simplification
•⁠  ⁠Reduce the number of simultaneous commitments where possible
•⁠  ⁠Identify tasks that can be delegated, simplified, or dropped

Mental recovery
•⁠  ⁠Allow yourself periods each day with no demands (no screen, no task)
•⁠  ⁠Engage in activities that require little decision-making to rest mentally
