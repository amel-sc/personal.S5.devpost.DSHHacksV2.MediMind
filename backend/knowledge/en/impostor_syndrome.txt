Impostor syndrome

Definition
A persistent feeling of not deserving one's achievements and fear of being "found out" as incompetent, despite objective evidence of competence.

Common signs and symptoms
•⁠  ⁠Attributing successes to luck or circumstances rather than one's own skills
•⁠  ⁠Intense fear of failure or judgment
•⁠  ⁠Systematically downplaying one's abilities
•⁠  ⁠Excessive work to "compensate" for a feeling of incompetence

Frequent causes
•⁠  ⁠Highly competitive environment
•⁠  ⁠Comparison with peers perceived as more capable
•⁠  ⁠Change of environment (new program, prestigious new institution)
•⁠  ⁠Very high personal standards

Suggested solutions

Awareness
•⁠  ⁠Note your skills and concrete results factually
•⁠  ⁠Distinguish the subjective feeling of incompetence from the objective reality of your abilities
•⁠  ⁠Remember that this feeling is common and shared by many high-achieving students

Sharing and discussion
•⁠  ⁠Talk about it with peers, who often feel the same way
•⁠  ⁠Ask for constructive feedback rather than judging yourself alone

Accepting mistakes
•⁠  ⁠Accept that mistakes are a normal part of learning
•⁠  ⁠Reframe failure as information rather than a judgment of personal worth
