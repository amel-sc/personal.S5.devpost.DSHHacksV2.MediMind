Sleep disorders

Definition
Difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or non-restorative sleep, common among students during periods of heavy workload or stress.

Common signs and symptoms
•⁠  ⁠Difficulty falling asleep despite being tired
•⁠  ⁠Frequent nighttime awakenings
•⁠  ⁠Non-restorative sleep, fatigue upon waking
•⁠  ⁠Shifted sleep-wake schedule (going to bed very late)
•⁠  ⁠Daytime sleepiness, difficulty concentrating

Frequent causes
•⁠  ⁠Stress, anxiety, rumination at bedtime
•⁠  ⁠Late-night screen use
•⁠  ⁠Irregular schedules (classes, social events, night work)
•⁠  ⁠Caffeine or stimulant consumption late in the day

Suggested solutions

Sleep hygiene
•⁠  ⁠Go to bed and wake up at fixed times, even on weekends
•⁠  ⁠Avoid screens at least 30 minutes before sleeping
•⁠  ⁠Create a calming bedtime ritual (reading, gentle stretching, soft music)
•⁠  ⁠Keep the bedroom dark, quiet, and cool

Daytime habits
•⁠  ⁠Limit long or late naps during the day (20 minutes maximum)
•⁠  ⁠Engage in regular physical activity, but not right before bedtime
•⁠  ⁠Reduce caffeine and alcohol later in the day

Mental management
•⁠  ⁠Avoid schoolwork or screens in bed
•⁠  ⁠Keep a notebook to jot down intrusive thoughts before sleeping
•⁠  ⁠Practice relaxation or slow breathing during nighttime awakenings
