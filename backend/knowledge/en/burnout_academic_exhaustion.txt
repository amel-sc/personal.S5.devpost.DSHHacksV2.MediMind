Burnout / academic exhaustion

Definition
A state of physical, emotional, and mental exhaustion resulting from prolonged exposure to excessive workload or stress without sufficient recovery.

Common signs and symptoms
•⁠  ⁠Intense exhaustion, even after resting
•⁠  ⁠Cynicism or demotivation toward studies
•⁠  ⁠Feelings of inefficiency or loss of meaning
•⁠  ⁠Irritability, progressive disengagement
•⁠  ⁠Physical symptoms (headaches, digestive issues, lowered immunity)

Frequent causes
•⁠  ⁠Prolonged work overload without breaks
•⁠  ⁠Lack of recognition or perspective
•⁠  ⁠Perfectionism or inability to delegate/let go
•⁠  ⁠No boundaries between academic and personal life

Suggested solutions

Reducing the load
•⁠  ⁠Reassess and reduce workload where possible (number of courses, extra activities)
•⁠  ⁠Learn to say no to additional requests
•⁠  ⁠Delegate or share certain group tasks

Recovery
•⁠  ⁠Allow yourself real breaks without guilt (days, evenings off from work)
•⁠  ⁠Reintroduce enjoyable activities unrelated to studies (hobbies, sports, creativity)
•⁠  ⁠Protect fixed periods of sleep and rest

Meaning and perspective
•⁠  ⁠Identify the specific sources of exhaustion and talk to a teacher or advisor about them
•⁠  ⁠Reconnect with the original reasons for choosing your field of study (intrinsic motivation)
•⁠  ⁠Review medium-term goals so they remain realistic

Support
•⁠  ⁠Reach out to a student support service or academic advisor
•⁠  ⁠Consider a temporary accommodation (extended deadlines, reduced load) if the institution allows it
